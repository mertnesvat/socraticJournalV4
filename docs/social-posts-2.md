The most counter-intuitive idea in all of breathing science. When people learn this, they can't un-know it. High share rate.

### Video 1 — "Breathing more gives you less oxygen. Here's the biology."

**Platform:** TikTok / Reels
**Length:** 45–55 seconds
**Hook type:** Counter-intuitive science + identity threat

---

**[0–3s] HOOK — on screen text + voiceover:**
> "If you've ever been told to 'just take a deep breath' when you're anxious — that advice is making things worse. Here's why."

---

**[3–10s] REVEAL:**
> "Your blood is almost certainly carrying enough oxygen right now. 98%, 99%. That's not the problem. The problem is whether your cells can actually receive it."

---

**[10–42s] SCIENCE (the payoff):**
> "Here's the thing they didn't teach you in school. Oxygen doesn't just float out of your red blood cells into your tissues. It needs a trigger — and that trigger is carbon dioxide.

> This is called the Bohr Effect. Discovered in 1904 by Danish physiologist Christian Bohr. When CO₂ rises in your blood, hemoglobin releases oxygen to your tissues. When CO₂ drops — like when you're over-breathing — hemoglobin holds on to the oxygen. Tightly. And your cells get less of it.

> So when you hyperventilate, you blow off CO₂. Your blood oxygen on a pulse oximeter looks great. But your brain and muscles are getting less oxygen than when you were breathing calmly.

> That tingling in your hands and face during a panic attack? The dizziness? That's not anxiety. That's the Bohr Effect. You've breathed yourself into a CO₂ crash.

> Fewer, slower breaths — not more — is the answer."
